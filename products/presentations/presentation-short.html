Opening Frame

AI Changes the Workflow Before It Changes the Work

AI can:

  • replace thinking
  • accelerate weak habits
  • support expertise
  • improve documentation
  • make research easier to inspect, verify, and continue

The question is not whether AI belongs in research.

The question is what kind of research workflow it creates.

Three Principles

Principle 1: Transparency

Good AI-supported research should let us reconstruct:

  • what we asked
  • what AI generated
  • what we accepted, rejected, or revised
  • what evidence supports each claim
  • what uncertainty remains

Transparency is not only disclosure to others.

It is also the ability to recover our own reasoning later.

Target: 3 minutes.

Make the internal/external distinction aloud rather than using a separate slide. Internal is more aligned with the goals of rigorous science

Principle 2: Validation

AI output is not evidence.

Researchers still have to validate:

AI can generate… Researchers must check…
summaries whether sources support them
citations whether they exist and matter
code whether it runs and answers the question
interpretations whether they follow from evidence
critique whether it is warranted

Target: 3 minutes.

This slide absorbs the former “Generation Is Not Validation” slide. Keep the phrase “candidate for review, not warranted knowledge.”

Principle 3: Constraint

Constraint means narrowing the task, context, and evidence AI is allowed to use.

Researchers constrain AI by specifying:

  • the research question
  • the relevant literature
  • the evidentiary standard
  • the analytic workflow
  • the claims that are permitted
  • the claims that remain uncertain

Constraint is one way expertise enters the workflow.

Target: 4 minutes.

Knowledge Integrity

From Principles to Practice

Principle Knowledge-integrity practice
Transparency preserve prompts, drafts, decisions, and sources
Validation check claims before reuse
Constraint curate the context AI can access

The aim is not just to produce artifacts faster.

The aim is to preserve the conditions under which knowledge can still be trusted.

Target: 3 minutes.

Use this as the hinge from abstract principles to the live project.

The Integrity Rule

Keep different kinds of material separate:

evidence             = sources, data, completed analyses
project knowledge    = checked claims worth reusing
process records      = drafts, plans, prompts, decisions

Before a claim becomes reusable knowledge, ask:

  • What supports it?
  • What is its status?
  • What limitation should travel with it?

Target: 4 minutes.

This is the memory hook for the workshop. In the live project, map these categories to sources/, data/, knowledge/, and ai/.

Provenance Questions

For any claim, ask:

  • Where did this come from?
  • Was it generated, copied, summarized, inferred, or verified?
  • What source or data supports it?
  • What changed between draft and final form?
  • What uncertainty remains?

Target: 3 minutes.

These questions become the audience’s lens for the demo.

Return Point

Closing Claim

AI strengthens research only when the workflow preserves:

  • expertise
  • evidence
  • transparency
  • validation
  • knowledge integrity

The useful question is not:

What can AI produce for us?

It is:

What kind of research practice does our use of AI make possible?
